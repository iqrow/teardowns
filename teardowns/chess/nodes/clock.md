term: Chess clock
aka:
  - clock
  - game clock
kind: part
plain: A chess clock is two linked time displays, only one of which runs at a time; after moving, a player presses it to complete the move and start the opponent's clock.
does: Makes thinking time a competitive resource and a formal condition of the game.
ask: Whose clock is running, and when is this move completed?
cues:
  - Two linked time displays
  - One display running
  - Clock pressed after a move
  - Flag on a display
children:
  - time-control
  - time-management
  - time-trouble
at: [0, 0.06, 0]
sources:
  - work: fide-laws-of-chess
    page: Article 6.1
    quote: "‘Chessclock’ means a clock with two time displays, connected to each other in such a way that only one of them can run at a time. ‘Clock’ in the Laws of Chess means one of the two time displays. Each time display has a ‘flag’."
  - work: fide-laws-of-chess
    page: Article 6.2.1
    quote: "During the game each player, having made his/her move on the chessboard, shall pause his/her own clock and start his/her opponent’s clock (that is to say, he/she shall press his/her clock). This ‘completes’ the move."
  - work: chesscom-chess-clocks
    page: "Chess Clocks: An Introduction"
    quote: "Just one of the two clocks runs at a time, with players starting their opponent's clock (and pausing their own) by pressing a button after making each move."

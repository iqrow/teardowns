A strong player sees a chess position as a few familiar patterns: a pawn structure, a castled king, a tactic waiting to happen. Each move, they read the position (material, pawns, king safety, piece activity), choose a plan that fits it, then calculate the forcing lines to check that the move is safe. The game has three phases: an opening that players often prepare at home, a middlegame of plans and tactics, and an endgame of exact technique. A clock limits the thinking time. Players improve by playing, going over their games, and studying master games and puzzles until the patterns are recognised at a glance.

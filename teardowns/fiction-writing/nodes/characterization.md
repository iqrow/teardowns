---
term: Characterization
aka: [showing character, bringing characters to life]
kind: process
plain: How the text shows who someone is. Burroway’s direct methods are appearance, speech, action and thought; authorial interpretation is the indirect one.
does: Lets readers build the person from evidence, so the person feels real.
ask: What does she do, say or notice that only she would?
trouble: The narrator says she is brave, but she never does anything brave.
at: [0.07, 0.62, 0.36]
links:

    items:

        name: Appearance (direct)

        sources:

            page: §4.5, Voice
            quote: "Do you favor certain kinds of clothing?"
      - id: method-speech
        name: Speech (direct)
        plain: What she says, how she says it, and what she avoids saying.
        sources:
          - work: openstax-writing-guide
            page: §4.5, Voice
            quote: "Do you have certain characteristic gestures or speech patterns?"
      - id: method-action
        name: Action (direct)
        plain: What she does while pursuing a goal. Checking a drip three times shows fear and care.
        sources:
          - work: openstax-writing-guide
            page: §4.5, Characters
            quote: "the way characters speak and behave"
      - id: method-thought
        name: Thought (direct)
        plain: Private thoughts, judgements and feelings that only the reader hears.
        sources:
          - work: openstax-writing-guide
            page: §4.5, Point of View
            quote: "the narrator also describes his feelings"
      - id: method-interpretation
        name: Authorial interpretation (indirect)
        plain: The narrator tells us what she is like. Quick, but the reader must take it on trust.
        sources:
          - work: openstax-writing-guide
            page: §4.5, Voice
            quote: "the way your words describe characters"

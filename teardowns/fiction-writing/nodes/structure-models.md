---
term: Structure models
aka: [story templates, beat sheets, three-act structure]
kind: knob
plain: Maps of where the big turns usually fall. Each model shows the pattern at a different level.
does: "Give you a checklist to diagnose a draft: what is missing, early or late."
ask: Which model fits my genre, and where do my turns fall on it?
trouble: A formula followed beat by beat; the story feels mechanical.
at: [0, -0.3375, 0.012]
links:
  - {to: planning, rel: see also}
variants:
  - label: Model
    items:
      - id: model-three-act
        name: Three acts
        plain: Setup to about 25%, the long middle to 75% with a midpoint near 50%, then the end. Weiland notes the timing is flexible.
        sources:
          - work: k-m-weiland-quick-guide-to-timing-plot-points
            page: Timing Plot Points 101
            quote: "The First Act (in which you’ll introduce characters, settings, and stakes) will take up the first 25% of your book"
      - id: model-save-the-cat
        name: Save the Cat! beats
        plain: "Brody’s 15 beats at set points: Catalyst 10%, Break into Two 20%, Midpoint 50%, All Is Lost 75%, Final Image 99–100%."
        sources:
          - work: jessica-brody-save-the-cat-writes-a-novel
            page: pp. 2–3, Introduction to the 15 Beats
            quote: "Catalyst (10%) - An inciting incident (or life-changing event)"
      - id: model-heros-journey
        name: Hero’s journey
        plain: Vogler’s 12 stages, from the ordinary world to the return with the elixir. A flexible skeleton, not a formula.
        sources:
          - work: christopher-vogler-a-practical-guide-to-joseph
            page: 1985 memo; The Stages of the Hero’s Journey
            flag: memo transcription hosted by Illinois Association of Chiefs of Police
            quote: "He reaches the INNERMOST CAVE where he endures the SUPREME ORDEAL."
      - id: model-seven-point
        name: Seven-point structure
        plain: "Wells: hook, plot turn 1, pinch 1, midpoint, pinch 2, plot turn 2, resolution. Plan backward from the resolution."
        sources:
          - {work: dan-wells-seven-point-story-structure-writing, page: transcript, quote: "Hook Plot Turn I Pinch I Midpoint Pinch II Plot Turn II Resolution"}
      - id: model-freytag
        name: Freytag’s pyramid
        plain: Five parts for drama, from introduction to catastrophe, with three crises on the way.
        sources:
          - work: gustav-freytag-technique-of-the-drama-trans
            page: ch. II, part 2, Five Parts and Three Crises
            flag: public-domain transcription; host editor notes duplication
            quote: "It rises from the introduction with the entrance of the exciting forces to the climax, and falls from here to the catastrophe."
      - id: model-kishotenketsu
        name: Kishōtenketsu
        plain: "Four parts: introduction, development, twist and conclusion. The third part, ten, brings an unexpected turn."
        sources:
          - work: shinsuke-tsuchiya-from-acting-out-stories-to
            page: pp. 357, 361–365 (kishōtenketsu)
            quote: "kishōtenketsu 起承転結 (introduction, development, twist, and conclusion)"

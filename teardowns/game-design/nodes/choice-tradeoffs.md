---
term: Trade-off structure
aka: [what I give up to get this]
kind: concept
plain: "A trade-off makes desirable outcomes compete: getting more of one costs something else. Reloading gains future ammunition but gives up the ability to fire during the reload."
does: Makes the best action depend on priorities and circumstances rather than a universally superior button.
ask: What valuable thing does choosing each alternative sacrifice?
children: [choice-opportunity-cost, choice-risk-stakes]
at: [0, 0.26, 0.01]
links:
  - {to: cost-curves, rel: see also}
  - {to: investment-capacity, rel: see also}
variants:
  - label: Trade-off pattern
    items:
      - id: choice-now-later
        name: Now versus later
        plain: Keep firing now or spend time reloading for the next engagement. Change the horizon; the same scarcity remains.
        why: "It counts when an immediate action competes with a future benefit, not because the action is firing or reloading."
        sources:
          - work: tynan-sylvester-decision-based-gameplay-design
            page: "Difficult Decisions: reload timing and buying now versus saving"
            quote: Should I reload now or later? What if the enemy comes around the corner while I’m reloading? But what if I run out of ammo while I’m fighting an enemy?
      - id: choice-safety-progress
        name: Safety versus progress
        plain: Stay in cover or expose yourself to take an objective or retrieve a better weapon.
        why: "It counts when safety competes with progress, not because cover or an objective is present."
        sources:
          - work: tynan-sylvester-decision-based-gameplay-design
            page: "Difficult Decisions; Examples: retrieving the dropped AK-47"
            quote: Should I move my position forwards, backwards, laterally, or not at all now? What if I get shot while I’m in between cover? But what if I lose because I don’t make my objective?
      - id: choice-attention-split
        name: Attention allocation
        plain: Manage the current fight or inspect an economy that may be falling behind. Attention itself is the scarce resource.
        sources:
          - work: tynan-sylvester-decision-based-gameplay-design
            page: "Difficult Decisions: RTS attention management"
            quote: A player may be micromanaging a battle, but his economy will be dying from lack of attention.
sources:
  - work: tynan-sylvester-decision-based-gameplay-design
    page: Difficult Decisions
    quote: At all times, the player must decide what his spending priorities are, where to focus his attention, how many resources to put into scouting, defense, offense, economic development, and so on.

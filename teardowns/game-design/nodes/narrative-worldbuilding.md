---
term: Worldbuilding
aka: [setting design, world model]
kind: process
plain: Worldbuilding defines the social, historical, economic and physical context that makes the game's actions make sense.
does: Gives mechanics consequences in the fiction and supplies motivations, constraints, conflicts and places for play.
ask: What kind of world would make this player action common, costly or meaningful?
cues:
  - Social or historical context in play
  - Economic or physical context around actions
  - Places that make actions meaningful
range: Mechanic-first or premise-first; local setting to whole culture; explicit lore to environmental implication.
trouble: The setting is a database of facts unrelated to play, or the rules let players act in ways the fiction cannot explain.
at: [-0.6, -0.2, 0.1]
sources:
  - work: emily-short-not-exactly-mailbag-worldbuilding
    page: Worldbuilding from a Mechanic
    quote: If you've got mechanics, that typically means you've got an action/set of actions for the player to perform
deeper: The next level would hold the player action, affected state, institutions, resources, history, beliefs and contradictions.
---

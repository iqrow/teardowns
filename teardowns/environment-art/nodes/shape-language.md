---
term: Shape language
aka:
  - form language
  - silhouette rules
  - shape vocabulary
kind: concept
plain: A repeatable preference for round, square, triangular or mixed forms in silhouettes and construction. Shapes give artists a shared vocabulary for communicating meaning.
does: Gives modelling and review a shared vocabulary for forms.
ask: Which base shapes should dominate, and where should a different shape create contrast?
at: [0.9, -0.32, 0.04]
cues:
  - Similar round, square or triangular forms recur in an asset
  - Curves, corners or points carry a distinct feeling or function
  - Mixed shapes create contrast in one silhouette
variants:
  - label: Base shapes
    items:
      - id: round
        name: Round
        plain: Curves, rounded edges and circular forms dominate the silhouette.
        why: The shared feature is round edges and curves; a friendly subject or organic material is not the reason.
        tint:
          round: '#c98e68'
          square: '#b9c9aa'
          triangle: '#9e6c5c'
        sources:
          - work: walt-disney-family-museum-shape-language
            page: Shape Language, p. 2
            quote: "Circles are organic and natural shapes. Round out edges and add curves to evoke a warm and welcoming feeling."
      - id: square
        name: Square
        plain: Squares, rectangles and grounded planes dominate the silhouette.
        why: The shared feature is square or rectangular blocking that creates weight and grounded or balanced forms; a building subject is not the reason.
        tint:
          round: '#7fa47f'
          square: '#c49460'
          triangle: '#6f7f66'
        sources:
          - work: walt-disney-family-museum-shape-language
            page: Shape Language, p. 3
            quote: "Using squares and rectangles to block in certain aspects of a drawing can give us the sense of weight and how grounded or balanced it is."
      - id: triangle
        name: Triangle
        plain: Points, tapers and wedge forms dominate the silhouette.
        why: The shared feature is pointed direction; a mountain or dangerous subject is not the reason.
        tint:
          round: '#7d9eaa'
          square: '#b18b6e'
          triangle: '#c36b52'
        sources:
          - work: walt-disney-family-museum-shape-language
            page: Shape Language, p. 4
            quote: "Triangles are sharp. Exaggerating the size and length of these shapes can heighten fear and transform how menacing the character is."
          - work: walt-disney-family-museum-shape-language
            page: Shape Language, p. 4
            quote: "When using triangles, the key word is point. How you use these points in terms of direction and quantity will suggest its functionality."
sources:
  - work: walt-disney-family-museum-shape-language
    page: Shape Language, p. 1
    quote: "Shape Language is a concept used in art and animation to communicate meaning based on shapes we are familiar with. When used in character, object, and background design, shapes can tell a story, show personality, and illicit an emotional response in the viewer without using any words."

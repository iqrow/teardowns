---
term: Mic distance
aka:
  - mic placement
  - how close the mic is
kind: knob
plain: How far the mic is from the mouth. Each halving of the distance raises
  the direct voice about 6 dB (inverse square law) while the room stays about
  the same; very close, a directional mic adds bass (proximity effect).
does: Sets the ratio of voice to room more than any setting in software.
ask: Can I get the mic closer?
cues:
  - "Voice-to-room balance"
  - "Boomy bass and pops when too close"
  - "Echo and noise when too far"
range: "Handheld or desk mic: about 15–30 cm (Shure); lav: about 20–30 cm from
  the mouth, on the chest (RØDE)"
trouble: Echo and noise (too far); boomy bass and pops (too close).
at:
  - -0.5398
  - 0.5
  - 0.3012
sources:
  - work: chris-lyons-audio-systems-guide-for-video-and
    page: Microphone placement, pp. 13–16
    quote: The poor sound you often hear on online videos is often recorded on the
      camera’s built-in microphone.
  - work: david-bauer-intensity-and-sound-level
    page: Inverse-square law
    quote: The intensity is inversely proportional to the square of the distance
      from the source, $I \propto 1/r^2$.
  - work: shure-microphone-techniques-for-live-sound
    page: Proximity effect
    quote: Proximity effect - With unidirectional microphones, bass response
      increases as the mic is moved closer (within 2 feet) to the sound source.

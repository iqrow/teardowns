---
term: "Split edit"
aka:
  - "J-cut"
  - "L-cut"
  - "pre-lap"
  - "sound bridge"
kind: "process"
plain: "A cut where sound and picture change at different moments: the next shot’s sound starts early, or the last shot’s sound runs on."
does: "Smooths a cut and carries the viewer across it. Sound that arrives first builds anticipation for the picture."
ask: "Should the next sound lead the picture, or the last one trail it?"
cues:
  - "A voice heard before its speaker appears"
  - "A line that carries on over the next shot"
  - "J and L shapes on the timeline"
range: "About half a second of lead for a subtle split, two to three seconds for a deliberate pull."
trouble: "Two sounds fighting in the overlap."
at: [0.16, 0.34, -0.338]
variants:
  - label: "Shape"
    items:
      - id: "j-cut"
        name: "J-cut"
        plain: "The next shot’s sound starts before its picture. Also called a pre-lap."
        sources:
          - work: "adobe-perform-j-cuts-and-l-cuts"
            page: "Perform J cuts and L cuts"
            quote: "J cut: In a J cut, the next scene’s audio plays before the image changes. This creates an anticipation effect and can be useful when introducing a new character or location before the visual cue appears."
          - work: "sami-ullah-what-is-a-prelap"
            page: "Prelap, defined"
            quote: "On a timeline the audio clip pokes out to the left of its video clip, which makes the shape of a J, so editors also call it a J-cut."
      - id: "l-cut"
        name: "L-cut"
        plain: "The last shot’s sound carries on over the next shot’s picture."
        sources:
          - work: "adobe-perform-j-cuts-and-l-cuts"
            page: "Perform J cuts and L cuts"
            quote: "L cut: In an L cut, the audio from the previous scene continues to play over the video of the next scene. This creates an overlapping effect, which helps maintain continuity and creates a smooth transition between scenes."
